# Who to contact first

## Best-fit profiles (need 200+ paid B2B invoices of history, ideally over 12-24 months, 15–60 day terms)
1. **Small agencies** (marketing, web, design, dev): few big clients, chronic late payers, owner watches cash weekly.
2. **B2B wholesalers / distributors** with dozens of trade accounts.
3. **Freelance collectives and consultancies** invoicing mid-size companies.
4. **Staffing / recruitment firms**: high invoice volume, thin margins.
5. **Bookkeepers and fractional CFOs**: they serve many SMEs, so one pilot can become a channel. Best long-term lever.

Weak fits: B2C shops (paid at checkout), businesses with fewer than ~200 paid invoices of history in total (the tool refuses to run below 200: too little for a trustworthy test).

## Where to find them (your own accounts, one personalised message each)
- Your existing network and past clients: ask who handles invoicing and chases payments.
- LinkedIn: search "founder" or "finance manager" + agency / wholesale; read their post history for cash-flow or late-payment complaints.
- Communities: accounting and freelancer forums, Indie Hackers, relevant Slack/Discord groups. Offer help in threads about late payments before pitching.
- Bookkeeper directories in your target country.

## Tracking (copy into a sheet)
Name · Company · Profile · Source · Date contacted · Reply · Pilot CSV received · Audit sent · Outcome

## Success targets for the first month
- 10 personalised messages sent → expect 1–3 replies.
- 1–2 CSV exports received → run `python -m latepay audit file.csv --client "<name>"`.
- 1 testimonial or paid audit. If zero replies after 25 messages, change the message or the profile, not the product.

## Rules
- No bulk or scraped mailing lists (spam and GDPR rules). Only people you can justify contacting for a business reason, with an easy way to say no.
- Never promise results. Show the arena report, including when it says "not predictable".
